TL;DR

The problem

Desk workers and students feel pain and discomfort but don't know why, and getting help usually means paying an expert.

What I did

Designed the app from lo-fi to hi-fi, built the front end in Xcode, and ran two rounds of testing that reshaped the measuring and results screens.

The outcome

A working iOS prototype that measures a desk in three steps and turns it into simple fixes. Recognized with an RGD Student Award Honorable Mention.

Overview

Your personal ergonomist, in your pocket.

ErgoScan measures your workspace with AR and analyzes it with AI, so you can fix your setup yourself. No expert, no cost.

What I did

  • UX/UI design, from lo-fi wireframes to hi-fi screens
  • Front-end prototype in Xcode (SwiftUI + ARKit), built with help from Claude
  • User testing and iteration

Diana led the concept, the UX flow and the backend, including the OpenAI API integration.

The problem

Most people don't know their desk is hurting them.

Many people spend 8+ hours a day at their desk.

Without realizing it, they adjust to their setup. They slouch a little more and strain a little further, and the damage adds up.

problem.pngProblem visual: person at a desk with strain points
16:9, 2400 × 1350 px

01

Can't see the problem

People know bad posture is harmful, but can't tell what's wrong with their own setup.

02

No clear first step

Even when they want to fix it, they don't know where to start.

03

Experts cost too much

Professional assessments exist, but they're too expensive and inconvenient for everyday use.

Process

How we got there

Exploration

What testing changed.

Two decisions where feedback from real people reshaped the design.

Challenge 1 · Measuring

Familiar pattern, fewer placement errors

Before
before-measure.pngOld measuring screen
phone, 1170 × 2532 px

Free tap to place points on a small camera view. During the in-class showcase, testers weren't sure where to tap and couldn't aim precisely.

After
after-measure.pngNew measuring screen
phone, 1170 × 2532 px

A crosshair and a plus button on a full-screen camera. People already know this from iPhone's Measure app, so there's nothing new to learn and fewer misplaced points.

Challenge 2 · Results

From a data dump to an actionable report

Before
before-results.pngOld results screen
phone, 1170 × 2532 px

Raw measurements in a flat list, colored by brand instead of status. Related info was spread across the screen, and each card had too much text to scan.

After
after-results.pngNew results screen
phone, 1170 × 2532 px

A score you can read at a glance, using traffic light colors (green, yellow, red). What needs fixing comes first, and each measurement lives in one card.

Final solution

Measure, understand, improve.

Measure

Three steps, one camera.

Viewing height, monitor distance and keyboard position, each measured by aiming and tapping. A quick two-question check covers elbows and wrists.

Know what's wrong first.

The score shows how your setup is doing. Anything that needs improvement comes first, with a specific fix and why it matters.

UX research · 2025

Stu-View usability test

A moderated usability study of GBC's Stu-View student portal, evaluating how well students complete common campus tasks.

Full write-up coming soon. The study ran with 5 Interaction Design students across 4 tasks (student awards, payment plan, photo ID reissue, class schedule). Task 2 had the most critical errors despite the shortest time, pointing to a misleading UI path. Key findings: card-based layout was hard to scan, top-level categories were ambiguous, and 3 out of 5 participants abandoned navigation and searched instead. Email me if you'd like the full report early.
